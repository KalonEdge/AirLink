## How it works

AirLink lets devices that are close to each other message and call directly, over Bluetooth and the local Wi-Fi network. There is no account, no server and no internet connection: messages and calls go from one device to the other and to nobody else. A message for someone out of range waits, and is delivered when they come back. Every device has an eight-character number derived from its own keys, so it can't be faked.

AirLink also finds things. Pick any Bluetooth device in range and walk towards it, with a live signal reading, a distance estimate and a tone that speeds up as you get closer.

## Requirements

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on a Mac with Apple silicon or Intel.

## Install

1. Download [AirLink.zip](https://github.com/KalonEdge/AirLink/releases/latest/download/AirLink.zip).
2. Open it and drag AirLink into Applications.
3. Open AirLink from your Applications folder. The first time, macOS asks for permission to use Bluetooth and the local network, and the microphone when you first make a call.

AirLink is signed by its developer and notarized by Apple.

## Updates

AirLink keeps itself up to date. It checks for a new version once a day, downloads it in the background and installs it when you quit, so it never interrupts a call. To check right away, choose AirLink › Check for Updates…. Automatic updates can be turned off in Settings › General.

Every version is listed, with what changed, on the [Releases](https://github.com/KalonEdge/AirLink/releases) page.

## Privacy

Messages and calls travel over Bluetooth and the local network, directly between nearby devices, and never to a server or the internet. Messages are sealed end to end and calls are encrypted. To be reachable, AirLink announces your display name and public keys to devices in range, and that announcement is not encrypted. Messages and contacts are stored on your Mac, with message text sealed by a key held in your Keychain. Finding devices only listens to broadcasts any device in the room can already hear. Checking for updates is the only time AirLink connects to the internet, and it sends only the version of AirLink you have.
